## Who to design for first

Recommended initial use model: Sigma Tactix delivers the service; the client uses a compact portal to provide context, review decisions and receive results. Small agencies are a secondary expansion segment, because agency-wide administration and scale add requirements beyond a first external client release.

All three profiles below are product hypotheses informed by the studies and the existing Growth OS use case. They are not people interviewed or statistical demographic segments.

| Profile | Role and buying relationship | Proposed recruiting firmographics | Job to be done | Psychographic hypothesis | Needed product behavior |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Founder / general manager | Economic buyer and occasional approver | UAE/GCC B2B services firm; propose 5-50 staff; active acquisition activity and authority to grant data access | Understand where growth is stuck and what the team will change next | Outcome-driven, short on time, wants leverage without losing control | One clear overview; commercial KPI definitions; three priority decisions; credible report and accountable owner |
| Marketing / performance lead | Daily operator and internal champion, including Sigma's own delivery operator | Small internal marketing team; propose 1-5 practitioners; uses ad/analytics tools and recurring reports | Convert scattered data and brand documents into coordinated work with less repetitive production | Values speed, accuracy and professional credibility; distrusts fabricated metrics and hidden work | VANTA in page context, traceable insights, reusable briefs, task ownership, recoverable failures and export-ready assets |
| Client sponsor / reviewer | Budget or brand approver, may not be technical | Existing pilot client's decision maker; company size follows buyer profile | Review the proposed change quickly and safely authorize it | Wants clarity about destination, cost, risk and what happens after approval | Plain-language preview; evidence; one approval/rejection decision; clear status; mobile-friendly review; no infrastructure jargon |

### Demographics and context

- Use role, decision rights, organization size, data maturity, recurring workflow and geography as initial segmentation dimensions.
- Do not invent target ages, gender, nationality, education or income. The research does not establish these for Growth OS. They are not necessary acceptance criteria.
- UAE/GCC is the launch-market hypothesis, based on founder reach and delivery context, not market share research.
- English-first, desktop production and mobile review are initial usability hypotheses. Ask about Arabic, RTL, localized currency, business timezone and report language. Do not claim Arabic coverage before testing.
- Recruit by pain: recent recurring report or asset production, multiple sources/documents, an approval step, and a named accountable owner. Avoid prospects who cannot grant access or identify a measurable outcome.

## What users probably want, in priority order

These are product inferences to validate, not direct customer quotes:

1. A trustworthy answer to what changed, why it matters and what to do next.
2. Execution that uses their actual business context and preserves ownership.
3. Less report/asset rework, with client-ready PDFs, slides and spreadsheets.
4. Predictable control of publishing, sending, spend and access.
5. A continuous history of decisions, work and results across sessions and devices.

The 20 November release should demonstrate these through a complete workflow. Model count, agent count and connector-logo count are not customer outcomes.

## Rapid validation before scope freeze

Use three 15-minute conversations: one buyer, one operator and one reviewer. A five-person task usability test can be added if accessible, but neither interview count establishes product-market fit. The founder must arrange conversations; no outreach is authorized or performed here. Test role tasks against the prototype while engineering investigates known defects independently.

Ask:

1. Walk me through the last client report or campaign deliverable you produced. Where did time and rework occur?
2. Which decision did the report help someone make, and what evidence did they need to trust it?
3. What could VANTA perform without checking with you, and what exact actions need approval?
4. Which two sources and file formats must work on day one, and who can authorize them?
5. Show how you would find an insight, inspect evidence, request a deliverable and approve it. What is unclear or missing?

Record observed behavior, role, workflow, source, severity and exact supporting evidence. Freeze or revise scope based on blockers to the shared workflow. Do not change the deadline scope because one prospect requests an unrelated platform.

## Traceable research-to-product decisions

| Hypothesis | Acceptance task | Outcome to record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Overview is understandable | Buyer identifies the most important issue and next action without explanation | Completion, confusion, evidence opened, time |
| VANTA carries context | Operator moves between report, source and work pages and asks VANTA to inspect the current artifact | Correct Client Enclave, current page/source attached, no cross-client exposure |
| Deliverables are usable | Reviewer opens PDF, PPTX and XLSX; checks named content, legibility and source period | Actual file opens, no clipping, editable slides, correct formulas/units, provenance |
| Delegation preserves control | Reviewer approves a version, changes it, revokes access and inspects the run | New approval for changed payload, revoked execution blocked, visible receipt/error |
